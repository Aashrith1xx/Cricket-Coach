The same shot filmed from in front. Adds hip–shoulder separation and stance width, and gives the 3D view its left–right axis. Without it the 3D skeleton stays flat, because one camera genuinely cannot see that axis.

The rating grades the checks, not you against other players. You'll get a score out of 100 on the mechanical checks this clip could support — sequence and direction, both settled without needing a reference population — plus measurements with error bars. What you won't get is "your knee is more flexed than typical": that needs reference ranges nobody has gathered, and inventing them would produce feedback that looks right and isn't.

For anything useful, the clip needs to be

  • Side on — square to the player, not at an angle. Joints have to be in the camera's plane to be measurable at all.
  • Still — phone on a tripod, a bag, a fence. Handheld gets rejected, because camera movement looks exactly like player movement.
  • Slow motion — 120fps or 240fps. At 30fps the moment of contact or release happens between frames.
  • Close enough — the player filling most of the frame height.
  • One person — in frame, for the whole clip.
